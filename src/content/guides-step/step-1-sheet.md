# Step 1: write your idea on one sheet

This guide takes you from a loose idea to a street-tested sheet.
The full sheet lives at [Your idea on one sheet](../../../idea-sheet/); here is the step by step to fill it this week.

## What you will achieve

- A 5-line sheet with your idea: problem, customer, what you sell, how they solve it today, and your edge.
- 5 to 10 talks noted with place and price they pay today.
- Your top 3 objections written down; they drive step 4 pricing.

## What you need from the previous step

- Nothing: this is the first step. Just bring your loose idea and 2 or 3 hours this week to go out and ask.

## Step by step

1. **Copy the 5 lines.** Open [Your idea on one sheet](../../../idea-sheet/) and finish one sentence per line: problem (who suffers what, when, where), customer (who pays, with place and moment), what you sell (what you deliver and at what price per unit), how they solve it today (where they go and what they pay), and your edge. If one is hard, that is your first gap: that is the question you take to the street.
2. **Look at the example once.** The sheet has a filled print-vending example next to a school and a hospital. Use it as a length guide (one sentence per line is enough); do not copy it.
3. **Go ask, not to please.** Talk to 5 to 10 possible customers where they are (in the example: outside the school and the hospital). Ask the sheet's 3 questions: where they printed last time and what they paid; what hurt (hours, line, price or distance); if a machine were there 24h at that price, would they use it this week.
4. **Note place and price.** From each talk keep where it happened and what they pay today. With no price of today there is no step 4 pricing.
5. **Pull your top 3 objections.** Count what repeats and write the 3 most common objections. They drive step 4.

Your **value proposition** (why they pick you over competitors, see [Glossary](../../../guide/glossary/)) comes from line 5 tested against what the street told you, not from your head.

## Where to look for free

- The street where your customers are: direct observation and hand-counting where they go today.
- The 5 to 10 talks: your best source. They cost time, not money.
- Prices on display at the businesses where they solve it today (in the example: the corner copy shop and the cyber across the street).
- When you move to step 2, open data joins in: INEGI DENUE and Google Trends (see the step 2 guide).

## Common mistakes

- Asking "do you like it?" instead of the 3 questions: people say yes to be nice and you learn nothing.
- Customer = "everyone": if you do not name place and moment, you do not know whom to find on the street.
- Paragraph-long sheet: if a line needs a paragraph, you do not understand that part yet. Cut it to one sentence.
- Not noting the price they pay today: with no number, step 4 pricing has nowhere to start.
- Jumping to market (step 2) without the 5 to 10 talks: the SOM is built on pages per person, which only come from those talks.

## Checklist

- [ ] 5-line sheet written with your idea.
- [ ] 5 to 10 talks noted with place and price they pay today.
- [ ] Top 3 objections written down.
- [ ] **Fills:** idea sheet. You will use it in the step 2 market study.

## Acceptance criteria (Given-When-Then)

- **Given** a beginner with a loose idea, **When** they follow this guide, **Then** they finish with a 5-line sheet + 5 to 10 talks noted with place and price.
- **Given** a sheet with one line that is hard to complete, **When** they apply the step by step, **Then** they spot their first gap and use the 3 interview questions to close it on the street.
- **Given** a finished sheet with top 3 objections, **When** they move to the next step, **Then** they carry today's price and the objections that feed the market study (step 2) and pricing (step 4).
