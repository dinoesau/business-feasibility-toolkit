# Step 8: stress-test your numbers

Calculator: [sensitivity](../../../calculators/sensitivity/).
Paper it fills: risk section of the study. Legal validity: Mexico only.

## What you will achieve

Know whether your business stays alive when something goes wrong, before spending on legal steps.
You finish with three written scenarios (pessimistic, base, optimistic) and a decision made in the same language as step 5: continue, adjust, or stop.
This is your second early exit: if the plan cannot survive a bad 20%, adjusting or stopping here saves money later.

## What you need from the previous step

- Your base profit from step 7: monthly revenue, fixed costs, and variable costs.
- Your price and variable cost per unit (steps 3 and 4).
- Your break-even point from step 5, to compare against the pessimistic scenario.

Without these numbers do not invent scenarios: go back to steps 3 to 7 and close your figures first.

## Step by step

1. **Write your base case.** Note three monthly numbers: revenue, fixed costs, and variable costs. Coffee-shop example: revenue $140,000, fixed $60,000, variable $49,000. Base profit: $140,000 - $109,000 = $31,000 (MXN).
2. **Build the three scenarios with one formula.** Per scenario:
   - scenario revenue = base revenue x (1 + sales %).
   - scenario costs = (fixed + variable) x (1 + costs %).
   - profit = scenario revenue - scenario costs.
   - margin = profit / scenario revenue.
3. **Use these shocks, not others.** Pessimistic: sales -20% and costs +10%. Base: no change. Optimistic: sales +20% and costs +5%. They match the calculator and the worked example, so anyone can reproduce your result.
4. **Run the numbers.** With the coffee-shop example: optimistic $168,000 - $114,450 = $53,550 profit; pessimistic $112,000 - $119,900 = -$7,900 loss. Repeat with your figures in the [sensitivity calculator](../../../calculators/sensitivity/).
5. **Test the price war.** In the calculator move price between -20% and +20% (9 scenarios) with your real price, variable cost, units, and fixed costs. If a -10% price turns your profit negative, your price cannot survive competition: record it as a risk.
6. **Apply the exit rule and write your decision.** Use the step 5 language:
   - **Continue:** the pessimistic case keeps profit >= 0 and break-even stays below your SOM.
   - **Adjust:** the pessimistic case loses money but you know which change fixes it (raise price, cut a fixed cost, replace an input) and you will test it within 30 days.
   - **Stop:** the pessimistic case loses money and no realistic adjustment fixes it, or pessimistic break-even needs more customers than exist. Stopping here is also winning.

## Where to look for free

- **Past input prices:** your own invoices from the last 6 to 12 months, or prices on MercadoLibre and the Central de Abastos. That shows whether +10% or +20% already happened before.
- **Slow months in your trade:** ask someone in the trade how much January or other slow months drop; compare with your -20% sales to check you were not too kind.
- **Competitor promos:** published menus and catalogs, Rappi or Uber Eats for food, and social media. If they discount 15% every month, your price-war scenario must include it.

## Common mistakes

- **Moving only sales and leaving costs still.** When you sell less, inputs often rise at the same time. Move both, as the formula does.
- **Borrowing optimism.** Writing +30% sales and 0% costs so "it closes". The shocks are fixed (-20%/+10% and +20%/+5%) so nobody cheats.
- **Confusing margin with profit.** Margin can look fine while profit is not enough to live on. Decide with profit in pesos, not the percentage.
- **Not writing the decision.** Three scenarios without a written continue/adjust/stop are useless. No written record, no decision, same as step 10.

## Checklist

- [ ] Base case written: monthly revenue, fixed and variable costs with source.
- [ ] Three scenarios computed with the formula (pessimistic, base, optimistic).
- [ ] Pessimistic scenario compared against the step 5 break-even point.
- [ ] Price war tested: profit at -10% and -20% price recorded.
- [ ] Decision written in step 5 language: continue, adjust, or stop.
- [ ] If adjust: concrete change, owner, and re-evaluation date (30 days max).
- [ ] Same figures in calculator, risk study, and (if you continue) business plan.

## Given-When-Then

- **Given** a -20% sales and +10% costs scenario, **When** you apply this guide in the calculator, **Then** you know whether your plan survives (profit >= 0) or must stop.
- **Given** a 10% price cut from a price war, **When** you test it across the 9 calculator scenarios, **Then** you know whether your margin survives competition or you must adjust costs before continuing.
- **Given** the three computed scenarios, **When** you apply the exit rule, **Then** a continue/adjust/stop decision is recorded with a date, without inventing new shocks.
