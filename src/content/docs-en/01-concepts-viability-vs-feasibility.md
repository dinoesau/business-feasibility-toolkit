# 01 - Concepts: viability vs. feasibility

> **Legal validity: Mexico only.** This is a faithful English mirror of the Spanish docs. Legal requirements described here apply exclusively to Mexico.

## 1. Short definitions

- **Viability:** is it worth it? Can the business work and last? Integral judgment (market, technical, financial, legal, organizational, environmental).
- **Feasibility:** can it be done? With what resources, timelines, permits, and concrete costs? Documented proof through studies.
- Rule of thumb: every feasibility study proves viability, but an idea that looks viable on paper may be unfeasible due to capital, permits, or technology.

## 2. The six viability types

| Type | Question | Proven in |
|---|---|---|
| Market / commercial | Are there customers who will pay? | Market study: demand, segments, competition, TAM/SAM/SOM |
| Technical | Can it be produced/delivered? | Technical study: process, capacity, equipment, location |
| Organizational | Is there team and structure? | Organizational study: org chart, profiles, HR |
| Legal | Is it allowed and registrable? | MX legal study: entity, RFC, permits, NOMs, IP (see `05-legal-framework-MX.md`) |
| Financial | Do the numbers work? | Financial study: investment, costs, NPV/IRR, breakeven, sensitivity |
| Environmental | Is the impact manageable? | Environmental study: baseline, impact matrix, mitigation |

## 3. Document flow and when to use each

1. **Idea sheet (1-2 pages):** quick filter. 14 sections: general data (SCIAN activity code in MX), description, market, competition, revenue, resources, channels, costs, team, SWOT, preliminary indicators, financing, milestones, annexes.
2. **Feasibility study (6 studies):** in-depth proof. Executive summary, introduction, the 6 studies, integral evaluation, annexes.
3. **Business plan (10 sections):** turns approved feasibility into an execution plan.
4. **Executive summary (1-2 pages):** written last, placed first. 10 points: company, problem, solution, market, model, traction, team, finances, use of funds, call to action.
5. **Pitch deck (10-15 slides):** visual version for investors (Sequoia-style template).
6. **Founders' agreement:** rules among partners (capital, 4+1 vesting, drag/tag-along, non-compete, arbitration). Mexico only in this toolkit.

## 4. Common mistakes

- Confusing the idea sheet with the plan: the sheet filters, it does not raise money.
- Building financials without market data: sales must come from the market study, not optimism.
- Leaving legal/environmental for last: in MX a denied land-use permit or uncertified NOM kills the project.
- Writing the executive summary first: always last, with closed and consistent figures across plan, summary, and deck.

## 5. Given-When-Then

- **Given** an unstructured idea, **When** the user completes the sheet, **Then** they get a preliminary verdict (proceed / adjust / discard) in < 2 pages.
- **Given** an approved sheet, **When** the 6 studies are completed, **Then** there is enough evidence for Go/No-Go.
